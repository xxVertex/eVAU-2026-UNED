Verb Tenses

Present Tenses

Present Simple
I work / She works
Hábitos, rutinas, verdades generales, horarios fijos. Marcadores: always, usually, often, sometimes, never, every day.
"She studies English every morning."
Present Continuous
I am working / She is working
Acciones en progreso ahora, tendencias temporales, planes futuros cercanos. Marcadores: now, at the moment, currently, today, this week.
"They are building a new school." / "I'm meeting her tomorrow."
Present Perfect Simple
I have worked / She has worked
Experiencias de vida, acciones con resultado presente, acciones recientes. Marcadores: ever, never, already, yet, just, recently, since, for.
"I have never been to Japan." / "She has just left."
Present Perfect Continuous
I have been working
Acción que empezó en el pasado y continúa (énfasis en la duración). Marcadores: for, since, all day, how long.
"He has been studying for three hours." / "How long have you been waiting?"
State verbs — no se usan en Continuous: know, understand, believe, think (= opinar), want, need, like, love, hate, seem, appear, contain, belong, own, cost, mean, remember, forget. → "I know" ✓ / "I am knowing" ✗
Since vs. For: Since + punto en el tiempo (since 2020, since Monday) / For + duración (for three years, for a long time).
Verb Tenses

Past Tenses

Past Simple
I worked / She worked (regular) · I went / She went (irregular)
Acción completa en el pasado, en un momento concreto. Marcadores: yesterday, last week/year, ago, in 1990, when.
"She visited Rome last summer." / "Did you see the film?"
Past Continuous
I was working / They were working
Acción en progreso en un momento del pasado; acción de fondo interrumpida por otra (past simple).
"While I was cooking, the phone rang." / "At 8pm she was studying."
Past Perfect Simple
I had worked / She had gone
Acción pasada anterior a otra acción pasada ("el más que perfecto"). Marcadores: already, just, before, after, by the time, when.
"When I arrived, the film had already started." / "She had never flown before."
Past Perfect Continuous
I had been working
Acción en progreso en el pasado hasta otro momento pasado (énfasis en la duración).
"He had been waiting for two hours when she finally arrived."

Used to / Would / Be used to / Get used to

EstructuraUsoEjemplo
used to + inf.Hábito o estado en el pasado que ya no existeI used to smoke. She used to live here.
would + inf.Hábito pasado repetido (solo acciones, no estados)Every Sunday, we would visit grandma.
be used to + -ingEstar acostumbrado a (hábito actual)I'm used to waking up early.
get used to + -ingAcostumbrarse a (proceso)She is getting used to living alone.
Verb Tenses

Future Forms

FormaUsoEjemplo
will + inf. Predicción sin evidencia presente, decisión espontánea, promesa, oferta It will rain tomorrow. I'll help you. I'll have the salad.
be going to + inf. Plan o intención previa, predicción con evidencia presente I'm going to study law. Look at those clouds — it's going to rain.
Present Continuous Arreglo o plan concreto con otra persona (futuro próximo) We're having dinner with them on Friday.
Present Simple Horarios fijos, programas, transportes The train leaves at 8:30.
Future Continuous (will be + -ing) Acción en progreso en un momento futuro; acción planificada (educado) This time tomorrow I'll be flying to London.
Future Perfect (will have + pp.) Acción que se habrá completado antes de un momento futuro By next year, I will have graduated.
Structures

Conditionals

TypeIf clauseMain clauseUse
Zero if + present simple present simple Verdades generales, hechos científicos
First if + present simple will + infinitive Condición real o posible en el futuro
Second if + past simple would + infinitive Situación imaginaria o poco probable en el presente/futuro
Third if + past perfect would have + past participle Situación imaginaria en el pasado (irreversible)
Mixed if + past perfect would + infinitive Condición pasada con consecuencia en el presente
Zero: If you heat water to 100°C, it boils. Si calientas agua a 100°C, hierve.
First: If it rains tomorrow, we will cancel the trip. Si llueve mañana, cancelaremos el viaje.
Second: If I had more money, I would buy a new car. / If I were you, I would apologise. Si tuviera más dinero, compraría un coche nuevo. / Si yo fuera tú, me disculparía.
Third: If she had studied harder, she would have passed the exam. Si hubiera estudiado más, habría aprobado el examen.
Mixed: If he had left earlier, he would be here now. Si hubiera salido antes, estaría aquí ahora.
Unless = if not: Unless you study, you will fail. = If you don't study, you will fail.
Otras conjunciones condicionales: provided (that), as long as, on condition that, supposing (that), in case.

Passive Voice

Formation: be (in the correct tense) + past participle
The agent (quien hace la acción) se introduce con by. Se omite si es desconocido, obvio o irrelevante.
TenseActivePassive
Present SimpleThey make cars here.Cars are made here.
Present ContinuousThey are building a bridge.A bridge is being built.
Past SimpleSomeone stole my bag.My bag was stolen.
Past ContinuousThey were filming a movie.A movie was being filmed.
Present PerfectThey have announced the results.The results have been announced.
Past PerfectThey had repaired the road.The road had been repaired.
Future (will)They will publish the book.The book will be published.
ModalThey must complete the form.The form must be completed.
InfinitiveSomeone needs to clean this.This needs to be cleaned.

Impersonal passive (It is said that… / He is said to…)

People say that she is brilliant. → It is said that she is brilliant. / She is said to be brilliant. Se dice que es brillante.
Verbos comunes: say, believe, think, know, consider, report, claim, expect, suppose, understand
It is believed that… / It is reported that… / She is expected to…

Relative Clauses

PronombreFunciónDefiningNon-defining
who / thatPersonas (sujeto o CD)✓who (no that)
which / thatCosas (sujeto o CD)✓which (no that)
whosePosesión (personas y cosas)✓✓

Defining vs. Non-defining
Defining (restrictiva): Identifica al antecedente. Sin comas. Se puede omitir el relativo si es CD: The book (that) I read was great.
Non-defining (explicativa): Añade información extra, no esencial. Con comas. No se puede omitir el relativo ni usar "that": My sister, who lives in London, is a doctor.
Defining: The man who/that called you is waiting outside. (identifica qué hombre)
Non-defining: My father, who is 60, has just retired. (información adicional)

Verbs with change of meaning

Verb+ Gerund+ Infinitive
rememberI remember locking the door. (recuerdo haberlo hecho)Remember to lock the door. (no olvides hacerlo)
forgetI'll never forget meeting her. (olvidar que algo pasó)I forgot to call him. (olvidé hacer algo)
stopHe stopped smoking. (dejar de fumar)He stopped to smoke. (paró para fumar)
tryTry adding more salt. (intentar como experimento)Try to finish by 5pm. (esforzarse en conseguir)
regretI regret telling him. (lamentar algo ya hecho)I regret to inform you… (lamentar comunicar algo)
meanThis means working harder. (suponer, implicar)I mean to finish today. (tener intención de)
Gerund obligatorio tras: preposiciones (interested in doing), after/before/when (after finishing), expresiones (it's worth doing, it's no use doing, there's no point doing).
Bare infinitive (sin to) tras: modals, make/let/help sb do, would rather, had better.

Writing

Essay Writing

For and Against Essay

ParagraphContentUseful phrases
IntroductionIntroduce the topic neutrally. State the debate.There is currently much debate about… / The issue of … has become increasingly important…
Arguments for2–3 points in favour with support/examplesOne of the main advantages is… / It is often argued that… / Supporters claim that…
Arguments against2–3 points against with support/examplesOn the other hand… / Critics argue that… / One major drawback is… / Nevertheless…
ConclusionBalanced summary or personal stanceTaking everything into account… / On balance… / To conclude… / In my view…

Opinion Essay

ParagraphContent
IntroductionState topic and your opinion clearly. In my opinion… / I strongly believe that… / It is my view that…
Body paragraphsEach paragraph = one main argument + example/evidence. First and foremost… / Another key point is… / Furthermore…
Counter-argumentAcknowledge and refute the opposite view. Although some people argue that… I believe… / While it is true that… nevertheless…
ConclusionRestate opinion, summary. In conclusion, I firmly believe that… / All things considered…
Writing

Formal Letter / Email

Structure of a formal letter
Salutation: Dear Mr/Ms/Dr [Surname], / Dear Sir or Madam, (si no sabes el nombre)
Opening: I am writing to… / I am writing with regard to… / I am writing in response to…
Body: Clearly structured paragraphs. One idea per paragraph.
Closing request: I would be grateful if you could… / I look forward to hearing from you.
Sign-off: Yours sincerely, (si sabes el nombre) / Yours faithfully, (si no sabes el nombre) + Full name.
